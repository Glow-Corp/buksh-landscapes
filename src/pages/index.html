Anatomy of a Buksh patio

The part you never see is why it lasts.

Edmonton ground freezes deep and thaws hard. Water trapped under a thin base turns to ice, expands, and lifts pavers. Here's how we build so it has nowhere to go.

Step 1 · Excavate & grade

Dig to firm ground, slope for drainage

We dig out to undisturbed soil and slope the subgrade away from your house, so meltwater runs off instead of pooling underneath.

Step 2 · Geotextile

Fabric between clay and gravel

A woven fabric keeps Edmonton clay from working up into the base. Invisible once it's done, and a big reason bases stay firm for years.

Step 3 · Compacted base

Crushed gravel, compacted in lifts

Placed and compacted layer by layer, not dumped in one go. This is what carries the load and resists frost heave. Typically 150–200 mm for a patio, deeper under driveways.

Step 4 · Bedding layer

Screeded perfectly level

A thin, even bed so every paver sits flat with no rocking and no low spots for water to collect.

Step 5 · Pavers & edging

Laid, locked, and pinned

Pavers set to pattern, edge restraint pinned so the field can't creep, and polymeric sand swept into the joints to lock it together and keep weeds out.

The result

Still flat after the spring thaw

A patio that drains, moves with freeze-thaw instead of breaking, and sits level for years. Backed by our 2-year workmanship coverage.

Depths shown are typical for a residential patio. Yours are set at the site visit, based on soil and use.

Just moved into a new build?

Get your final grade approved, the first time.

In Edmonton the builder usually handles rough grade. Final grade is the homeowner's job, and it has to pass the City's lot grading inspection. Many builders hold a landscaping deposit until it does.

01
Rough grade is approved

Usually done by your builder and signed off by the City.

02
We place and shape topsoil

To the elevations on your approved lot grading plan, sloped away from the foundation.

03
Certificate and inspection

A surveyor certifies the grades and the City inspects. Certification is guaranteed: if our grading doesn't pass, we make it right.

04
Sod goes down

The same crew lays your new lawn once grades pass. You're finished.

Deadlines and deposit terms depend on your builder and the City of Edmonton's lot grading rules. Bring your paperwork to the site visit and we'll walk you through it.

Questions

Straight answers.

Can't find yours? Call {{phoneDisplay}}. We pick up 7 days a week.

What does your warranty cover?

Hardscapes (patios, walls, steps) carry two years of workmanship coverage from completion. Turf and plant material are covered for one year from installation. If something related to our workmanship comes up, like a paver or wall moving because of how it was installed, we fix it.

Normal settling of new fill or backfilled foundations, frost movement outside our control, and problems from missed watering or pests aren't covered.

Do you handle final grading and the City inspection?

Yes. We do rough and final grading for homeowners and homebuilders, with certification guaranteed. If our grading doesn't pass, we make it right. Then we can lay your sod.

Can I finance my project?

Yes, through our partner Financeit. You can spread the cost into monthly payments. Ask about it at your site visit.

When should I book?

Edmonton's build season runs roughly May through October, and spring fills up fast. The best time to book a design visit is winter or early spring, so your project is planned and scheduled before the ground thaws.

What materials do you use?

Paving products, soil mixes, plants, turf and aggregates from established suppliers known for quality, chosen to suit each project and our climate.

Which areas do you serve?

Edmonton and the surrounding communities. If you're just outside the city, call and ask. We probably cover you.
